1. Asymmetric Spatial Model & 2×2 Phenology Matrix

NOAA GHCND / Open-Meteo + USFS FIA IDW (k=6)
Why Tree Species Are Spatially Interpolated While Weather Is Local (Asymmetric Default): Forest inventory plots (USFS FIA Phase 2/3) represent ecological sample stands, so tree species abundances (speciesMix, treesPerAcre) are interpolated across the k = 6 nearest stations using Haversine geographic distance (km) and an elevation-similarity Gaussian kernel. Meteorological forcing, however, is resolved directly at the target coordinate via 106 co-located NOAA GHCND stations, a 646-node PRISM 4km grid, live Open-Meteo GFS/HRRR forecasts, ERA5 Historical Reanalysis (1950–2025), and NASA SRTM 90m DEM elevation lapse rates (-3.5°F / 1,000 ft).

The engine evaluates a clean 2×2 Orthogonal Matrix crossing Axis 1 (Phenology Anchor: w/ vs. w/o Satellite/NPN Calibration) with Axis 2 (Pigment & Trajectory Kinetics: Simple Fixed Schedule vs. Complex Diurnal ΔT + CDD):

// 1. Haversine + Elevation-Gaussian IDW Weights across k=6 Nearest USFS FIA Plots
w_k = exp(-|Elev_k - Elev_site| / 2200 ft) / max(12 km, d_Haversine(k))^2.2  |  W_k = w_k / Σ w_j
// 2A. Simple w/ Phenology Calib ('calibrated') — Empirical Anchor + Fixed Schedule
DOY_simple_calib = DOY_base_NPN_VIIRS + 1.15·ΔT_anom - 1.2·(ΔElev / 400 ft)  |  Schedule: [-18d, -7d, 0d, +9d]
// 2B. Simple w/o Phenology Calib ('weather_only') — Pure Weather Anchor + Fixed Schedule
DOY_photo = 291.0 - 1.45·(φ - 41°)  |  DOY_simple_wx = DOY_photo + 1.55·(T_mean - 44.5°F) + 4.5·Moist - 0.14·(Sun - 65)
Schedule: [-22d, -9d, 0d, +13d]  |  Zero station/satellite peakDoyBase training data
// 2C. Complex w/ Phenology Calib ('diurnal_dynamic') — Empirical Anchor + Diurnal ΔT/CDD
CDD_14 = Σ max(0, 47°F - T_min,k)  |  ΔCDD_anom = CDD_climo_14 - CDD_14  [No double-counting of baseline chill]
DOY_complex_calib = DOY_simple_calib + 0.55 · clamp(0.045 · ΔCDD_anom, -6d, +6d)
// 2D. Complex w/o Phenology Calib ('complex_weather_only') — Pure Weather + Diurnal ΔT/CDD
DOY_complex_wx = DOY_simple_wx + 1.05 · clamp(0.065 · (115 - CDD_14), -9d, +9d)
v_chill = clamp(((48 - T_night)/(48 - T_night,climo)) · (0.82 + 0.18·(ΔT / 18°F)), 0.55, 1.65)
  • Baseline Alignment Guarantee: Under normal station climatology (ΔT ≈ 18°F, ΔCDD_anom = 0), Simple w/ Phenology Calib and Complex w/ Phenology Calib share the exact same calibrated peak date and baseline anthocyanin multiplier (~1.03–1.05×), diverging only when diurnal swings, chilling anomalies, or frost events depart from normal.
  • Genetic Onset Offset (δ_species,i): Early-senescing species turn up to 8 days before stand peak (Quaking Aspen -8d, Black Tupelo -7d, Red Maple -6d), while marcescent oaks and cypress peak 8–12 days later (White Oak +9d, Bald Cypress +12d).

2. Leaf Pigment Biochemistry & Climatology-Normalized Diurnal Sugar-Trap

Chlorophyll · Carotenoid · Anthocyanin · Tannin

Autumn leaf color is governed by four biochemical pigment classes. While Carotenoids & Flavonols (yellow/gold in Birch, Aspen, Hickory, Tulip Poplar) are always present in plastids and unmasked as chlorophyll degrades, Anthocyanins (scarlet, crimson, and purple in Maples, Oaks, Sweetgum, Tupelo) are newly synthesized in autumn when warm daytime photosynthesis (T_day ~ 55–70°F) pairs with crisp, non-freezing nights (T_night ~ 33–43°F) to trap sucrose in the leaf blade:

// Simple Modes ('calibrated', 'weather_only'): Night-Low Threshold Step Function
γ(T_night) = 1.15 if 32°F ≤ T_night ≤ 45°F  |  0.85 if <32°F  |  0.78 if >45°F
Step_A = (SunnyPct_eff / 70) · γ(T_night) · (1 - DroughtPct / 140)
// Complex Modes ('diurnal_dynamic', 'complex_weather_only'): Normalized Diurnal Sugar-Trap
f_day(T_day) = exp(-((T_day - 59.0°F) / 17.5°F)^2)  [Daytime photosynthetic sugar synthesis]
f_night(T_night) = exp(-((T_night - 38.5°F) / 8.5°F)^2)  [Chilled phloem trap; 0.42× penalty if <28.5°F]
f_swing(ΔT) = clamp((T_day - T_night) / 18.0°F, 0.50, 1.48)  [Normalized to 1.0× at standard ΔT = 18°F]
SugarTrap = 1.22 · (SunnyPct_eff / 70) · (0.38 + 0.62 · f_day · f_night) · f_swing · (1 - DroughtPct / 140)
Window (d_i) Pigment Stage Color Interpolation Rule
d_i ≤ -18d Chlorophyll Dominant 100% Summer Canopy green
-18d < d_i ≤ -7d Carotenoid Unmasking lerp(green, early, (d_i + 18)/11)
-7d < d_i ≤ 0d Peak Anthocyanin/Gold lerp(early, targetPeakHex(A_boost), (d_i + 7)/7)
0d < d_i ≤ +9d Tannin Oxidation lerp(targetPeakHex, lateBronze, d_i / 9)
d_i > +9d Senescent Umber Fades toward weathered bark/litter #5C4738

Crown Projection Area & Stand Density Weighting

Stem count alone misrepresents canopy color because a mature White Oak (112 m² crown) or Sugar Maple (88 m²) occupies nearly 3× the overhead area of a Quaking Aspen (38 m²). Each species' visual weight is:

W_base,i = (AbundancePct_i / 100) × CrownAreaM2_i
W_retained,i = W_base,i × Retention_i(d_i)
CrownRadius_px = √(CrownAreaM2_i) × 1.65

In the overhead simulation, tree crown count scales with treesPerAcre × canopyClosure, and crowns are rendered with 8-lobed shadow bases, mid-canopy foliage, and top-left solar highlights.

Allometric Height, Bark & Crown Architecture

The horizontal forest profile models a side-elevation transect (0–105 ft vertical scale at 1.92 px/ft) with depth-sorted trees (z ∈ [0.72, 1.06]) and five distinct botanical crown geometries:

  • Tall Dome / Dome: Tulip Poplar (92 ft), Sugar Maple (78 ft), American Beech (74 ft)
  • Spreading Broad Crown: White Oak (85 ft, 112 m²), Northern Red Oak (82 ft), Bigleaf Maple
  • Columnar Lenticel Bark: Quaking Aspen (52 ft) & Paper/Yellow Birch (62 ft) with pale bark (#D8D6CE) and dark horizontal lenticels
  • Conical / Pyramidal Tiers: Western Larch (88 ft), Evergreen Spruce/Fir/Pine (84 ft), Bald Cypress (80 ft)

Weibull Abscission & 1m × 1m Botanical Litter

Post-peak leaf retention follows a stretched-exponential Weibull decay governed by species marcescence factor ρ_i (e.g. Oaks ρ = 1.40 hold leaves late; Aspen ρ = 0.85 drops rapidly):

τ_i = 13 · ρ_i · (1 - DroughtPct / 110)
R_i(d_i) = exp( -((d_i - 2) / τ_i)^1.65 )
Dropped_i = W_base,i · (1 - R_i) → 65% Bark + 35% Litter

On the 1m × 1m ground quadrat, fallen leaves are drawn at 1.95 px/cm true physical scale (3.5 cm Larch needles to 22 cm Bigleaf Maple blades), layering older tannin-cured leaves (#6E472B) beneath freshly abscised leaves.

How peakfolio. Infers Exact Fall Foliage Palettes

Standard foliage maps reduce the entire continental canopy to a single 5-step status (No Change → Patchy → Partial → Peak → Past Peak). That hides the most important visual truth: "Peak" in an Aspen grove in Colorado is 70% luminous canary yellow (#F9D423) and 30% dark conifer green, whereas "Peak" in Vermont's Green Mountains is 60%+ scarlet and flame orange (#C8232C, #EA580C).

1. Species × Crown Area Weighting

Each region stores tree species distributions alongside average mature crown projection area (m²) and stand density (trees/acre). A massive Mature White Oak or Sugar Maple crown (95 m²) contributes more visual pixels to the canopy palette than an understory Dogwood or Vine Maple (18 m²).

2. Leaf Pigment Biochemistry

As photoperiod shortens, Chlorophyll (green) degrades, unmasking Carotenoids & Flavonols (yellow/gold) present in Birch, Aspen, Hickory, and Poplar. In Maples, Oaks, Sweetgum, and Tupelo, crisp sunny days + cool nights (35–45°F) synthesize Anthocyanins (crimson/scarlet/purple).

3. Weather & Elevation Phenology

Latitude, elevation (Hopkins' Bioclimatic Law: ~4 days earlier per +1° latitude or +400 ft elevation), chilling degree days, solar radiation, and soil moisture shift both timing and hue saturation.

4. Canopy Closure & Leaf Abscission

As senescence progresses past peak or wind/drought stress rises, deciduous species shed foliage, increasing the visible ratio of evergreen conifers, tannin bronze oak litter, and dormant bark/understory.
